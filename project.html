01 When exploding stars crash into their own gas

Circumstellar Interaction in Hydrogen-poor Supernovae

First author·Louisiana State University·Advisor: Dr. Emmanouil Chatzopoulos·2022–2026

Codes
MESA · STELLA · SuperLite · Duperfit · Python
Scale
30 models: 1 baseline, 13 wind, 9 inner-shell, 4 detached-shell and 3 hydrogen-rich variants
Output
First-author paper under review at ApJ · talk on an earlier version at AAS 243

The question

Hydrogen-poor explosions come in many forms: normal Type Ib/c supernovae, interacting Ibn/Icn-like events, fast blue optical transients (FBOTs), fast-evolving luminous transients (FELTs) and Type I superluminous supernovae (SLSNe-I), which can be 10–100× brighter than normal. Collision of the ejecta with circumstellar material (CSM) that the star lost before exploding is one way to power the bright end. I asked how the structure, extent and composition of that CSM move a single stripped-envelope explosion across this landscape, and whether any configuration matches both the light curves and the spectra.

Approach

  • Produced a hydrogen-free progenitor self-consistently through binary mass transfer in MESA, evolved it to core collapse and exploded it.
  • Built a 30-model grid of CSM environments: steady winds, compact shells next to the star, massive detached shells at ~1015 cm, and hydrogen-rich control versions.
  • Computed light curves with the radiation-hydrodynamics code STELLA and synthetic spectra with the Monte Carlo radiative-transfer code SuperLite.
  • Compared models and observed transients using the same light-curve metrics, the brightness–timescale–temperature–radius (BTTR) diagram, and Duperfit template matching.

My contribution

This is my project from start to finish, carried out under my advisor’s guidance: I built the binary progenitor and all 30 CSM models, ran every simulation on LSU’s HPC clusters, wrote the Python analysis pipeline, and wrote the paper.

02 Supernova code for GPU supercomputers

AresPK – Simulating Type Ia Supernovae on Heterogeneous HPC Architectures

Co-Design Summer School, summer 2023·Los Alamos National Laboratory (CCS-7, now CAI-1)·Team of 4: L. Dyken, A. Holas, M. I. Ugalino, N. Zaman

Stack
C++ · Kokkos · Parthenon (AMR) · MPI · Python
Hardware
Chicoma supercomputer: AMD EPYC CPUs and NVIDIA A100 GPUs
Output
Poster LA-UR-23-29212 · open-source code

The question

Type Ia supernovae are thermonuclear explosions of white dwarfs and are used as standardizable distance markers in cosmology. Simulating them means resolving physics over many orders of magnitude in length scale, which calls for adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and very large machines. Most existing codes were written for CPU-only systems, while new supercomputers get most of their power from GPUs. Our goal was a performance-portable code that runs efficiently on both.

Approach

  • Built AresPK on the Parthenon AMR framework and AthenaPK hydrodynamics, with Kokkos providing a single code base for CPUs and GPUs.
  • Added the physics needed for thermonuclear explosions: a nuclear statistical equilibrium (NSE) burning solver, a monopole self-gravity solver, and the Helmholtz equation of state, the first implementation of it to run on GPUs.
  • Ran strong- and weak-scaling studies on Chicoma, varying MPI ranks, CPU cores and GPUs.

Key results

  • Perfect strong-scaling speedup up to 8 GPUs, and near-linear scaling for both CPU and GPU ranks.
  • On average, a rank with one A100 GPU was 34% faster than a rank with 64 CPU cores.
  • Scaled to a 1024³ mesh on 512 CPU ranks (32,768 cores); weak-scaling efficiency dropped by about 55% at that largest scale, pointing to global communication as the next bottleneck.

My contribution

Wrote and validated the NSE burning solver, helped integrate the physics modules, and contributed to the scaling studies on Chicoma.

Technical deep dive

Why performance portability

A white dwarf is thousands of kilometres across, while the burning front that unbinds it is orders of magnitude thinner. AMR concentrates resolution where it is needed, but even then the runs need leadership-class machines, and those are increasingly GPU-based. Rather than maintain separate CPU and GPU versions, AresPK is written once against Kokkos, which compiles the same kernels for OpenMP on CPUs or CUDA on GPUs. Parthenon handles the block-structured mesh, load balancing and MPI communication.

Physics modules

  • NSE burning solver (my contribution): computes the equilibrium isotope mass fractions of burning material, checked for densities around 107 g cm−3 and temperatures of 6×109 K and across the temperature range relevant for thermonuclear burning.
  • Helmholtz equation of state: pressure and internal energy of a degenerate, equal-mass carbon–oxygen mixture; the first GPU implementation of this equation of state.
  • Monopole self-gravity: instead of a full Poisson solve, uses the spherical symmetry of the problem: mass is binned radially and the gravitational acceleration evaluated for every zone.

Scaling-study setup

The benchmark is an ideal-gas sphere evolved for 1000 cycles with AMR enabled. Each MPI rank uses either 64 OpenMP-enabled CPU cores or one A100 GPU, and the number of ranks is varied from 1 to 64 for strong scaling and up to 512 for weak scaling. The weak-scaling efficiency loss at the largest runs comes from global communication, which is where future optimization should focus.

03 How spinning stars throw off their outer layers

Rotational Dynamics in Pulsational Pair-instability Supernovae: Implications for Mass Loss and Transient Events

Co-author·Louisiana State University·T. N. Huynh, E. Chatzopoulos & N. Zaman·ApJ 991, 12 (Sept 2025)

Codes
MESA · STELLA · SuperLite · Duperfit · Python
Scale
33 rotating models of 85 M☉ and up, at 0.1 Z☉ and Z☉, rotation up to 70% of break-up
Output
The Astrophysical Journal 991, 12 (2025)

The question

Stars with helium cores of roughly 32–65 solar masses become unstable to electron–positron pair production late in their lives. Instead of exploding at once, they go through violent pulses that eject shells of material: pulsational pair-instability supernovae (PPISNe). When a later shell catches an earlier one, the collision can power a bright transient. We asked how stellar rotation changes the pulses, and what the colliding shells would look like to a telescope.

Approach

  • Evolved very massive stars with MESA at rotation rates of 0–70% of break-up, following every pulse through to core collapse.
  • Measured the number, energy, velocity, timing and composition of the ejected shells and how they correlate.
  • For a rotating 95 M☉ model, followed the collision of its first two shells with STELLA and computed its spectra with SuperLite, then compared them with observed supernovae using Duperfit.

Key results

  • Faster rotation reduces mass loss in the pulses: an 85 M☉ star at 70% of break-up ejects 2.95 M☉, compared with 7.5 M☉ at 20%.
  • Rotation lowers the mass threshold: a rotating 90 M☉ star builds a 55 M☉ helium core and pulsates, which a non-rotating star needs ~123 M☉ to do.
  • Across the 33 models, ejected mass and shell energy are tightly correlated (r ≈ 0.95), while faster rotation goes with less ejected mass (r ≈ −0.64). Later pulses are hydrogen-poor and carbon/oxygen-rich (62% oxygen in the second pulse of the 95 M☉ model), and come only ~26 days apart, so collisions are likely.
  • The two-shell collision produces a moderately luminous hydrogen-poor transient peaking at ~4.8–6.3×1042 erg s−1 and staying bright for ~50 days: similar to Type Ib/c supernovae such as SN 1994I and sharing some spectral features with SLSNe-I, but fainter, so the brightest SLSNe likely need an extra power source such as a magnetar.

My contribution

I did the spectroscopic analysis: ran the SuperLite radiative-transfer simulations on LSU’s HPC cluster, produced and plotted the synthetic spectral series, and compared them with observed supernovae using Duperfit.

Technical deep dive

Stellar models

Models were evolved with MESA r24.03.1 from the zero-age main sequence to core-helium depletion and then through the pair-instability phase with MESA’s HLLC hydrodynamics, which switches on when the star approaches dynamical instability. All unbound ejecta are kept and followed through later pulses. Convection uses the Ledoux criterion with exponential overshooting, and winds follow the Brott et al. (2011) prescription. Rotation rates of Ω/Ωcrit = 0, 0.2, 0.5 and 0.7 were explored at 10% solar metallicity, plus a few solar-metallicity models.

Shell collision light curve

The first and second ejections of the rotating 95 M☉ model are mapped into STELLA. The first pulse is evolved for 100 days; the second is then propagated until it reaches the first, assuming homologous expansion, and the collision is followed with radiation hydrodynamics. The first pulse (0.78 M☉, ~1.8×1049 erg) would peak near 1041 erg s−1; the second (4 M☉, ~7.8×1049 erg), launched ~26 days later, catches it within ~14 days, and the collision produces the brightest of the three signals, more than a century before core collapse.

Spectral synthesis (my part)

STELLA profiles from 10 days before to 30 days after peak were truncated at a Rosseland optical depth of 100 and post-processed with SuperLite using ~106 (220) Monte Carlo source particles and 6000 wavelength groups from 1 to 30,000 Å, with NLTE level populations for hydrogen. Before peak the spectra show broad C II and O II emission and a P-Cygni feature near 6600 Å; after peak, as the ejecta cool, absorption from O IV, Mg I, O V/N V and C II takes over.

Comparison with observed supernovae

Duperfit, a Python implementation of the Superfit template method, fits a spectrum as supernova plus host-galaxy light with reddening. I applied artificial redshifts of 0.01–0.1 to the model spectra and searched 0.03 ≤ z ≤ 0.17 with AV from −2 to 2. The closest templates include SN 2005bf and SN 1994I before peak, the SLSN-I PTF12dam and SN 2007bi after peak, and the fast transients AT2018cow and SN 2002bj, which share He I/He II lines. These are nearest matches for context, not quantitative best fits.

04 What powers the brightest supernovae?

Constraining the Powering Mechanisms of Superluminous Supernovae

PhD research, in progress·Louisiana State University·Advisor: Dr. Emmanouil Chatzopoulos·Started Jan 2026

Codes
TigerFit · MESA · STELLA · SuperLite · Python
Data
Public light curves of nearby SLSNe-I, starting with SN 2018bsz and SN 2017egm
Status
Ongoing

The question

What powers SLSNe is still debated: a rapidly spinning magnetar, interaction with circumstellar material, large amounts of radioactive nickel, or a mix. Semi-analytic light-curve fits often allow more than one answer. I am combining such fits with physically consistent progenitor and explosion models to narrow down the power source event by event. This builds directly on the two projects above.

Approach

  • Fit observed bolometric light curves with TigerFit, a semi-analytic fitting tool, to get first estimates of ejecta mass, explosion energy and CSM properties.
  • Build MESA progenitors consistent with those estimates.
  • Run STELLA and SuperLite with different circumstellar environments and test which ones reproduce the observed light curves and spectra.

Current status

  • First targets are SN 2018bsz and SN 2017egm, two of the nearest known SLSNe-I, with well-sampled light curves.
  • Results will be added here as the project progresses.

My contribution

Leading the project: light-curve fitting, model building and simulations.

05 Faster quantum-chemistry calculations

Accelerating the Fermi-Löwdin Orbital Descriptor Optimization for Self-Interaction-Free DFT Calculations

M.S. thesis, 2021·Central Michigan University, Department of Physics·Advisor: Dr. Juan Peralta, with Dr. Koblar Jackson

Code
FLOSIC (Fortran) · Python
Systems
HCN, C2H4, CH3F
Output
M.S. thesis (2021)

The question

Approximate density functional theory (DFT) suffers from self-interaction error. The Fermi-Löwdin self-interaction correction (FLO-SIC) removes it using orbitals defined by Fermi-orbital descriptors (FODs), whose positions must be optimized. That optimization is expensive because the SIC potentials are recalculated at every step. Could we skip that recalculation?

Approach

  • Developed a Frozen Potential (FP) variant of the FLOSIC code: the SIC potentials from the end of the self-consistent-field calculation are held fixed during FOD optimization.
  • Compared the resulting approximate FOD forces with the exact FLO-SIC forces.
  • Built force landscapes for HCN, C2H4 and CH3F using both optimal and non-optimal reference potentials.

Key results

  • Approximate forces go to about zero at the optimal FOD positions, like the exact forces.
  • Force-evaluation run time dropped by 72–86% (72.0%, 82.5% and 85.7% for the three test cases).
  • Limitation: for some bond and lone-pair FODs the approximate force has the opposite slope, so it cannot simply replace the exact force in a standard optimizer.

My contribution

Implemented the Frozen Potential method in the FLOSIC code, ran and analyzed all calculations, and wrote the thesis.

Technical deep dive

Why FODs are expensive

In FLO-SIC, each occupied orbital is replaced by a Fermi-Löwdin orbital built from a point in space, its FOD. The total energy depends on where the FODs sit, so they are optimized by following the “FOD forces” dE/dai downhill. In the standard code every force evaluation needs a new set of orbital-dependent SIC potentials, which dominates the cost, especially for molecules with many FODs where a run can take days.

The Frozen Potential idea and how it was tested

The FP code reuses the SIC potentials from the end of the self-consistent calculation and evaluates approximate forces for displaced FODs without regenerating them. To test it, I moved one FOD at a time through 10 positions along a chosen axis and computed exact and approximate forces at each, giving a force landscape. This was done with reference potentials taken both at the optimal FOD positions and at non-optimal ones, for core, bond and lone-pair FODs in all three molecules.

What it showed, and what comes next

For many FODs the approximate force tracks the exact one and crosses zero at the right place. For some bond and lone-pair FODs it crosses zero at the right place but with the opposite slope, so a standard minimizer could be pushed the wrong way. The thesis proposes transforming the forces (for example with a Lorentzian) so that zeros always correspond to minima, together with a compatible approximate target function. It is a proof of concept for faster FOD optimization, with its limits clearly mapped.
