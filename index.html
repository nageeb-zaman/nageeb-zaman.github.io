About Me

I'm a computational scientist finishing my PhD in physics at Louisiana State University (expected May 2027). My research asks why some exploding stars shine 10 to 100 times brighter than ordinary supernovae. To find out, I build computer models of massive stars and their explosions, run them on supercomputers, and compare the simulated brightness and colors with what telescopes actually observe.

Along the way, I have:

  • run a grid of 30 explosion models using more than 300,000 CPU core-hours, leading to a first-author paper in The Astrophysical Journal (under review);
  • written the nuclear-burning solver for AresPK at Los Alamos National Laboratory, a supernova code built for GPUs. On one GPU it ran 34% faster than on 64 CPU cores;
  • cut the run time of a quantum-chemistry calculation by 72–86% for my master's thesis at Central Michigan University.

I'm looking for full-time roles starting in 2027 where simulation, numerical methods and data analysis solve real problems, whether in scientific R&D, at a national laboratory, or in industry.

Outside research, I still code for fun: Conway’s Game of Life, the Mandelbrot set and the Sierpinski gasket are some of my favorite small projects. I also play badminton and cricket, and these days my favorite pastime is spending time with my daughter.
